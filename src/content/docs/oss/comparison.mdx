Giskard OSS combines two parts of a code-first testing workflow for AI engineers:

- [**Checks**](/oss/checks) expresses expected agent behavior as executable scenarios and pass/fail checks.
- [**Scan**](/oss/scan) discovers security and quality failures, using the same scenario and check model so you can save generated suites and maintain them as regression tests.

Checks and Scan are currently in beta. The comparison below focuses on their documented open-source capabilities and on the main open-source alternatives: [garak](https://docs.garak.ai/), [promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/), [PyRIT](https://azure.github.io/PyRIT/), and [DeepEval](https://deepeval.com/docs/getting-started).

## Why Giskard's dynamic Scan is different

Giskard Scan starts from the system you describe. Its generators create scenarios relevant to that system, run them, and attach executable checks to the results. A vulnerability scan looks for adversarial failures; a quality scan uses your knowledge base to look for incorrect or unsupported behavior.

The target does not have to be a chatbot. Checks can record broad serializable inputs and outputs, while Scan calls an async Python wrapper with typed Pydantic inputs and outputs. This lets you test structured systems, tool-using agents, or one step of a larger workflow. Multi-turn scenarios can keep state across interactions.

Scan is the discovery layer rather than the end of the workflow. Save `result.suite`, review and refine the generated scenarios, then commit the suite as a Checks regression test. You can replay it locally or with pytest and keep it in CI. Engineers remain in control of which generated scenarios become maintained tests.

## When another tool may fit better

- Choose **garak** when you want a specialist vulnerability scanner with a broad catalog of named probes against model or generator interfaces.
- Choose **promptfoo** when a configuration-driven CLI workflow and its provider, assertion, or red-team plugin ecosystem fit your stack.
- Choose **PyRIT** when you want a Python framework for composing custom attack orchestrations, targets, converters, memory, and scorers.
- Choose **DeepEval** when evaluation-metric breadth or its evaluation and red-teaming ecosystem is the main requirement.

These tools can also complement Giskard. For example, Giskard can run garak through [`third_party_scan`](/oss/scan/how-to/third-party-scanners) and expose its findings through the same result interface used by the built-in scan.
