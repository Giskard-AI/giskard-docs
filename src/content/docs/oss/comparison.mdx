Giskard OSS combines two parts of a code-first testing workflow for AI engineers:

- [**Checks**](/oss/checks) expresses expected agent behavior as executable scenarios and pass/fail checks.
- [**Scan**](/oss/scan) discovers security and quality failures, using the same scenario and check model so you can save generated suites and maintain them as regression tests.

Checks and Scan are currently in beta. The comparison below focuses on their documented open-source capabilities and on the main open-source alternatives: [garak](https://docs.garak.ai/), [promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/), [PyRIT](https://azure.github.io/PyRIT/), and Confident AI's paired [DeepEval](https://deepeval.com/docs/getting-started) and [DeepTeam](https://deepeval.com/docs/red-teaming-introduction) projects.

## Why Giskard's dynamic Scan is different

Giskard Scan starts from the system you describe. Its generators create scenarios relevant to that system, run them, and attach executable checks to the results. A vulnerability scan looks for adversarial failures; a quality scan uses your knowledge base to look for incorrect or unsupported behavior.

The target must be an LLM application or agent, but it does not need a chat-shaped interface. Scan generators produce textual test prompts, then map them into the input fields declared by your Pydantic model before calling the async target. This lets Scan exercise structured LLM applications and tool-using agents. Checks separately supports broader serializable inputs and outputs. Multi-turn scenarios can keep state across interactions.

Scan is the discovery layer rather than the end of the workflow. Save `result.suite`, review and refine the generated scenarios, then commit the suite as a Checks regression test. You can replay it locally or with pytest and keep it in CI. Engineers remain in control of which generated scenarios become maintained tests.

## When another tool may fit better

- Choose **garak** when you want a specialist vulnerability scanner with a broad catalog of named probes against model or generator interfaces. You can also [run garak through Giskard Scan](/oss/scan/how-to/third-party-scanners#run-garak).
- Choose **promptfoo** when a configuration-driven CLI workflow and its provider, assertion, or red-team plugin ecosystem fit your stack.
- Choose **PyRIT** when you want a Python framework for composing attacks, executors, targets, converters, memory, and scorers.
- Choose **DeepEval + DeepTeam** when you want Confident AI's evaluation-metric breadth together with its separate red-teaming framework.

These tools can also complement Giskard. Its [`third_party_scan` adapters](/oss/scan/how-to/third-party-scanners) run garak and DeepTeam against a Giskard target and normalize their findings into `SuiteResult`. DeepTeam is a separate project from DeepEval. Promptfoo, PyRIT, and DeepEval currently run separately against the target; Giskard does not normalize their results into `SuiteResult`.
