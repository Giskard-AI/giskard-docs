Giskard OSS combines two parts of a code-first testing workflow for AI engineers:

- [**Checks**](/oss/checks) expresses expected agent behavior as executable scenarios and pass/fail checks.
- [**Scan**](/oss/scan) discovers security and quality failures using the same scenario and check model; you can serialize generated suites and maintain them through your own regression-test workflow.

Checks and Scan are currently in beta. The comparison below focuses on their documented open-source capabilities and on five projects with overlapping evaluation or red-teaming scope: [garak](https://docs.garak.ai/), [promptfoo](https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/), [PyRIT](https://azure.github.io/PyRIT/), and Confident AI's paired [DeepEval](https://deepeval.com/docs/getting-started) and [DeepTeam](https://deepeval.com/docs/red-teaming-introduction) projects.

## Why Giskard's dynamic Scan is different

Giskard Scan starts from the system you describe. Its dynamic generators create scenarios relevant to that system, while other generators load fixed datasets. Scan runs those scenarios with executable checks. A vulnerability scan looks for adversarial failures; a quality scan can use a supplied knowledge base to look for incorrect or unsupported behavior.

The target is a typed callable; it does not need a chat-shaped interface or a particular agent class. Dynamic generators can emit the declared Pydantic input type directly. Dataset-backed and third-party generators instead map textual prompts into that input schema before calling the target. This lets Scan exercise structured applications, including user-wrapped tool-using systems, through their external callable boundary. Multi-turn scenarios can keep state across interactions.

Scan is the discovery layer rather than the end of the workflow. `result.suite` exposes the generated suite in memory; you can serialize it separately, edit it in Python, and replay it locally. Storage, version control, pytest integration, CI scheduling, and selection of maintained scenarios remain user-managed rather than native Giskard services.

## When another tool may fit better

- Choose **garak** when you want a specialist vulnerability scanner with a configurable catalog of named probes against model or generator interfaces. You can also [run garak through Giskard Scan](/oss/scan/how-to/third-party-scanners#run-garak).
- Choose **promptfoo** when a configuration-driven CLI workflow and its provider, assertion, or red-team plugin ecosystem fit your stack.
- Choose **PyRIT** when you want a Python framework for composing attacks, executors, targets, converters, memory, and scorers.
- Choose **DeepEval + DeepTeam** when you want DeepEval's maintained evaluation-metric families together with its separate DeepTeam red-teaming framework.

These tools can also complement Giskard. Its [`third_party_scan` adapters](/oss/scan/how-to/third-party-scanners) run garak and DeepTeam against a Giskard target and normalize their findings into `SuiteResult`. DeepTeam is a separate project from DeepEval, although the DeepTeam adapter uses DeepEval internally. Giskard has no selectable Promptfoo, PyRIT, or standalone DeepEval adapter and no product-owned normalization path for their standalone results.
