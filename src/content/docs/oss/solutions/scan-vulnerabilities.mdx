`vulnerability_scan` red teams your LLM agent: it attacks the agent on purpose to find safety and security failures. From a plain-language description, an LLM generates hostile scenarios tailored to your agent, such as [prompt injections](/start/glossary/security/injection) hidden in realistic content or requests for [harmful content](/start/glossary/security/harmful-content), and runs them as single messages and as multi-turn conversations. A second LLM then acts as a judge, reading each conversation and deciding whether the response reveals a vulnerability.

The judge is an LLM, so it is sometimes wrong in both directions. Read the failing conversations. And a scan that finds nothing means these scenarios did not break your agent, not that your agent is safe.

Unlike benchmarks that evaluate a foundation model generically, the scan performs an **in-depth, domain-specific assessment** of _your_ agent, based on the description you provide.

Install the package and configure a model, then point the scan at your agent:

```python
from giskard.scan import vulnerability_scan

suite_result = await vulnerability_scan(
    target=my_agent,
    description="A Q&A agent that answers questions about our product.",
    languages=["en"],
)
```

While the suite runs, Giskard shows live progress for each scenario, with a count of how many passed and failed:

The v3 equivalent of `giskard.scan(model, dataset)` is `vulnerability_scan(target=..., description=..., languages=[...])`. There is no dataset to prepare, no `giskard.Model(...)` wrapper, and no `model_type` or `feature_names` to declare: you wrap your agent as an async function and describe it in plain language. For finer control over which generators run, use the lower-level `generate_suite(...)` and then `suite.run(...)`.
:::

## What data is sent to Language Model providers?

The scan uses an LLM both to **generate** adversarial scenarios and to **judge** your agent's answers. Those models receive your agent's description and the responses it produces. They never receive anything you do not pass to the agent, and you choose the provider and model in [Install & Configure](/oss/solutions/installation).

## Beyond the open-source scan

The scan on this page runs locally against your own LLM provider and keys, and is driven by code. The [Giskard Hub](/hub/ui/scan), our enterprise platform, manages the complete red teaming workflow: through the web interface, the [Python SDK](/hub/sdk), or the API, you launch a more advanced scan (55+ probes across 11 vulnerability categories), get a security grade for your agent, and turn the findings into [test datasets](/hub/ui/datasets) that your whole team, including business experts, can review and annotate. [Continuous red teaming](/hub/ui/continuous-red-teaming) keeps testing your deployed agent against emerging threats, catching vulnerabilities and regressions before they can be exploited.

## Roadmap

`giskard.scan` is the single entry point for automated testing in the open-source library. `vulnerability_scan` red teams your agent; `quality_scan` probes answer quality against a [`KnowledgeBase`](/oss/solutions/reference/knowledge-base) of your own documents, generating hallucination, sycophancy, out-of-scope, and multi-topic scenarios. Both ship today. We keep expanding the attack library with new vulnerability categories and richer multi-turn attacks. New scenarios are picked up automatically by the call you already wrote, so nothing on this page changes as coverage grows.
